I am an Associate Professor of Information Technology and Operations Management in the College of Business at Florida Atlantic University, where I also serve as Director of the Supply Chain Management Program. I received a Ph.D. in Business (Business Information Technology), with a secondary major in Disaster Risk Management, from the Pamplin College of Business at Virginia Tech. I was named the College of Business Scholar of the Year in 2023.

My research examines how organizations and public agencies make decisions under uncertainty, with a growing focus on artificial intelligence. One stream studies AI-enabled decision making and risk: how humans and AI systems assess risk together, including the correlated blind spots that arise when both rely on the same frames, and how organizations should invest to secure enterprise AI systems against threats such as data poisoning. A second stream studies global supply chain risk and resilience, including how the political stability or instability of suppliers’ countries shapes buyer firms’ performance and how firms absorb and recover from disruptions. I also work on humanitarian and disaster operations and critical infrastructure, such as equity–efficiency trade-offs in infrastructure investment and the coordination of pandemic response policies across regions. I combine analytical models, empirical analysis of archival data, and large-scale computational methods. My work has been published in Production and Operations Management, European Journal of Operational Research, Decision Sciences, Risk Analysis, International Journal of Production Economics, and Regional Studies.
